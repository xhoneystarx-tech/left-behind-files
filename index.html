The Left Behind Files

Since the 1960s NASA has left landers, rovers, a helicopter and even mirrors on the Moon and Mars. A hydrogen atom detective named Hy is on the case.

Open the case

Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. Every date, distance and coordinate comes from NASA sources, listed at the end.

It was a quiet night in the solar system. Too quiet.

The name's Hy. Hy Drogen. One proton, one electron, zero days off. I've been around since just after the Big Bang, and I get everywhere. I'm in the water you drink, the stars you wish on, and the rocket fuel that pushed people to the Moon.

A kid about your age walked into my office with a question. People sent machines to the Moon and Mars. Where are they now? Did anybody bring them home?

Nobody brought them home. They are still out there, right where they stopped. Some are still working. Some went quiet years ago. One was never heard from again.

Every one of them left clues about how the Moon and Mars work. Grab your notebook, partner. We're opening the files.

      Experiment: the mirrors

      Measure the distance to the Moon with a laser

      Astronauts left trays of glass prisms on the Moon. Each prism sends light straight back where it came from. Fire a laser at one, time the echo, and you know how far away the Moon is.

      Earth and Moon sizes are to scale. The gap between them is not: 30 Earths would fit in it.

      3D is unavailable in this browser, but the timer still works.

      Aim at a mirror
      Closest to EarthFarthest
      The pulse travels in real time.
      Echo time 0.000 s Distance = speed of light × time ÷ 2
      • The Apollo 11 and Apollo 14 mirrors have 100 prisms each. Apollo 15's has 300.
      • Timing the echo gives the distance to within about a millimeter.
      • The measurements show the Moon drifts 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year. That is about how fast your fingernails grow.
      • In 2025 the Blue Ghost lander delivered a new NASA mirror, and it has already returned laser light.

          Project
          The Left Behind Files
          Challenge
          NASA Space Apps Challenge: tell the story of the hardware NASA has left across the solar system, for school-age space enthusiasts.
          Team
          Quantumers. Kien Do, Vietnam (team owner). Making space easy and fun for kids.
          The problem
          Sixty years of machines sit on the Moon and Mars, and most students have never heard what they are, where they are, or what they taught us.
          Our solution
          A detective story with real NASA coordinates on 3D globes, NASA's 3D spacecraft models, a laser experiment, and Hy, an animated hydrogen atom guide who answers questions with AI.
          How it was made
          We are hoping to make learning about space easy and fun for children. This website was built with the help of Claude.
